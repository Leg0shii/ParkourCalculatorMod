# No-turn global solver design record

## Ruling: the one biggest hammer

**Combinatorial-Benders / one-tree no-good-cut structure-pool enumeration over the discrete schedule (combos, sprint latch, gate pattern, min-edges as the master objective), with each proposed structure certified to gap=0 by the existing single-quadratic spatial branch-and-bound (`DiskSocpKernel` disk + arc chord/tangent cuts + exact 3-way gate branch in `CertifiedBnb`) replayed through `ExactJumpModel`, and seeded cold by a Scholtes wall-homotopy continuation front end.**

That is one hammer with three welded parts: an outer combinatorial master, an inner certified continuous oracle, and a cold incumbent front end. No single named textbook solver is the whole thing, and no off-the-shelf global solver (COPT/SCIP/Gurobi) is even a drop-in, because the two failures that actually kill this problem live outside what they provide.

### Why it dominates for THIS problem

The problem is a mixed-integer program with exactly one nonconvexity (the circle a^2+b^2=1), a min-edges objective, global landing walls, a state-triggered inertia gate, a free start, and a finite-lattice byte-exact truth. Match each structural fact to the survey verdicts:

- **Single quadratic => the continuous subproblem is EXACT, not merely relaxed** (single-quadratic survey, S-lemma / TRS; Yakubovich, Adachi-Nakatsukasa, Jeyakumar-Li). For a fixed schedule the node is an extended TRS: one disk plus linear walls plus free-start. Its optimum saturates the circle (measured abNorm 1.0000000003) and is recovered in closed form / one SOCP solve. So none of the exponential hardness lives in the geometry. This is precisely what `DiskSocpKernel` + `CertifiedBnb` already are. That kills the wide-arc enumeration that ended the beam/MITM cold solver: a bound replaces enumeration over the facing.

- **The residual hardness is combinatorial (schedule) and dead-zone (gate), and min-edges is the master objective.** The gcs-switched survey is decisive here: GCS is the wrong hammer (the shared circle G1 and the global walls G2 are not edge-local, so GCS's perspective tightness does not survive, and min-transitions is degenerate Viterbi on our instance). Its own verdict names **logic-based / combinatorial Benders (Hooker-Ottosson; Codato-Fischetti)** as THE hammer for min-edges specifically: a pure combinatorial master (no continuous vars) whose objective is the number of input changes = constrained total variation (Zeile-Sager), coupled to a certified continuous slave that returns IIS-strengthened no-good cuts. Codato-Fischetti targets exactly our gate/combo/latch big-M logic.

- **DP/reachability is dominated** (dp-reachability survey, own verdict DEAD): friction amplification 6.85..10.05 against 1e-4..1e-6 corridors forces ~1e-5 velocity quantization (~5e9 cells/tick) and dedup is unsound because the objective is not a function of the interface state. The R(theta)*S factorization removes theta from the state but converts the DP into injective schedule enumeration, not a compressed value function. DP contributes two components only: the friction-funnel admissible bound and free-start-as-seed-box.

- **The certificate must be byte-exact against a finite 65536-lattice oracle, not a continuous relaxation tolerance** (certified-incumbents survey). Continuous gap=0 is the wrong certificate: the byte-exact outcome is piecewise-constant per 0.00549 degree bucket, and the continuous optimum sits up to ~12560 buckets from the byte optimum cross-family. `ExactJumpModel` replay at FEAS_TOL=0 is the sole certificate; the relaxation only produces the bound and a candidate lattice point. This is the VIPR "verify the answer, not the solver" split, realizable in pure Java because the bound side is our SOCP kernel and truth is the exact replica.

- **Cold incumbent finding is the measured killer, and wall homotopy is the only thing that beat it cold** (certified-incumbents survey B; NOTURN-HANDOFF 4c). COPT found 0 incumbents in 500k+ nodes on the exact circle. Feasibility pump does not apply: the infeasibility is a corridor gap, not an integrality gap. Scholtes relax-then-tighten continuation (`WallHomotopyLadder`) is the measured cold rediscovery mechanism.

So: the geometry is polynomial and already solved (S-lemma exact node), the objective is native to a combinatorial Benders master (min-edges = TV shortest path), the gate is an MPVC that must be branched not rounded (already built in `CertifiedBnb`), and the certificate is exact replay. The composite is the only architecture that fits all five facts at once, and every piece but the outer driver already exists in pure Java.

## Architecture

### Layering

```
OUTER   structure-pool master        min-edges TV shortest path over the 9-combo x sprint trellis
        (combinatorial Benders)       + assignment + sprint-latch + edge-cap/dwell rows
                                       + accumulated IIS no-good cuts
          | proposes schedule sigma          ^ returns feasibility + objective, or active-wall IIS
          v                                   |
INNER   certified continuous slave     one extended-TRS node per sigma:
        (single-quadratic spatial B&B)  DiskSocpKernel disk + arc chord+tangent cuts + linear walls
                                        + SOC-RLT wall x disk rows, IPM weak-duality bound
                                        + CertifiedBnb arc bisection + 3-way gate branch (ZERO/POS/NEG)
                                        + free start px/pz
          | candidate lattice facing         ^ gate pattern fold fixed point (2 rounds)
          v                                   |
TRUTH   ExactJumpModel replay, FEAS_TOL=0    gap=0 iff disk/arc bound over the residual lattice
        on the 65536 sine grid               window meets a replay-verified incumbent
FRONT   WallHomotopyLadder continuation      cold first incumbent per structure (relax walls by delta,
END     + switching-cost-aware rounding      tighten to 0, fold gate each rung); SCARP -> low-edge start
```

### Node relaxation (inner slave), exploiting the single quadratic

For a fixed schedule the node is the extended TRS

```
min c . (a, b, p0)   s.t.   a^2 + b^2 <= 1,   W . (a, b, p0) <= d
```

- **Disk, not McCormick.** Enforce a^2+b^2<=1 as the SOCP disk (the S-lemma convex hull for one quadratic), never a bounding-box RLT on a^2+b^2. `DiskSocpKernel` already does this and emits the weak-duality certificate as the node lower bound.
- **Arc chord + tangent cuts.** `SineTableGeometry` already carries the Coffrin-Hijazi chord cos(mu)a + sin(mu)b >= cos(Delta). Add the two endpoint tangent cuts a cos(theta_e) + b sin(theta_e) <= 1 so the arc envelope is the convex hull (chord = inner, tangents = outer). Bound closes geometrically, ~8-12 bisections to snap to a grid bucket.
- **SOC-RLT wall x disk rows.** The walls generically cross inside the disk (a landing X-wall and a landing Z-wall, free-start corners), so the raw eTRS relaxation reopens a tiny gap (measured 2.1e-7..1.6e-3). Append the second-order-cone RLT product of each active wall with the disk (Anstreicher; Burer-Yang non-intersecting condition) so the node bound is certified-exact rather than empirically-tight. Cheap: one extra SOC row per wall in `DiskSocpKernel`. The fixed-m arc-face argument (Bienstock) makes this polynomial since only 1-2 walls are active at the optimum.

### Min-edges objective handling

Master objective = number of schedule transitions, encoded as a constrained total variation over the trellis:

```
assignment    sum_k x[t,k] = 1
edge var      c[t] >= x[t,k] - x[t+1,k],   c[t] >= x[t+1,k] - x[t,k]
minimize      sum_t c[t]          ties broken by the physics objective (MAX/MIN X at landing)
sprint latch  s[t] >= s[t-1] + fwd[t] - 1,   s[t] <= fwd[t]      (single-engage; H8)
edge cap      sum_t c[t] <= E_cap;   optional min-dwell L
```

With no cuts this is a min-TV shortest path solvable exactly by Viterbi DP in O(n * 9^2), pure Java. GCS is rejected for this (gcs-switched 2.2): the shared circle and global walls have no GCS home, and min-transitions is degenerate. The edge-cap MILP / TV encoding is the right home. Each Benders no-good cut is a small forbidden sub-assignment (a DFA); the product with the trellis stays a DP, degrading to a light custom B&B only when cuts accumulate.

### Gate-pattern handling

The per-axis 0.005 dead-zone is a state-triggered vanishing constraint (MPVC). Two-tier:

1. **Fold fixed point (incumbent-driven).** Relax-solve, replay, extract the realized gate pattern, refold the affine model with pattern-aware velocity walls, repeat. Converges in 2 rounds. This is `GateFoldFinder` + `FoldReplayDriver`. It must enforce both the zero side AND the keep-alive side; the COPT fold enforced only the zero side and the replay caught the rest.
2. **Exact 3-way branch (certificate closer).** On the residual low-velocity tail, branch each gate event ZERO / POS / NEG with `keepAliveWall`. This is already implemented in `CertifiedBnb` (byte states 1/2/3, interval propagation fixing POS when lo>=thr, NEG when hi<=-thr, ZERO inside (-thr,thr)). Rounding provably cannot fix a dead-zone (Sager-Bock-Diehl CIA is affine-in-control only; a single mis-fired gate is amplified ~11x by friction), so this branch is mandatory, not optional. A replay that violates a folded gate reopens it into the 3-way branch (premature-commitment guard).

### Incumbent engine (cold front end)

- **Wall homotopy continuation** (`WallHomotopyLadder`): relax landing/neo walls by delta (~0.05), solve clamp-free, tighten delta -> 0 in staged rungs, folding the gate pattern each rung. Scholtes relax-then-tighten; small rungs near active-set flips, restart on path loss at bifurcations. This is the measured cold incumbent source; it seeds the master's first incumbent per structure.
- **Switching-cost-aware rounding (SCARP)** turns a relaxed/fractional homotopy trajectory into a LOW-edge integer schedule (not just any feasible one), respecting the simplicity metric. It is a MIP-start factory, not a certifier.
- **Costate dual and feasibility pump are NOT incumbent engines here** (corridor-not-integrality; costate is warm-start only, violates walls 0.08..12.29 blocks). The costate/friction-funnel value serves only as an anytime prune bound inside the inner B&B.

### gap=0 certification

Declared when the disk/arc/SOC-RLT bound over the remaining lattice window meets a replay-verified incumbent. Acceptance test for any candidate is `JumpConstraintCompiler.maxViolation <= 0` on `ExactJumpModel` at FEAS_TOL=0. The master's LP/DP bound is a valid lower bound on edges throughout (a fractional 2.12 proves >= 3 edges). Termination: the sine grid is finite and every IIS no-good removes at least one lattice schedule, so the outer loop terminates at a certified global min-edges optimum; the RENS bucket-window leaf enumeration inside the inner solve is small (<= 3 buckets per ambiguous tick on modern chains) and each candidate is replayed.

## Runner-up hammer

**A monolithic single-quadratic spatial branch-and-bound over the whole (a,b, free-start, combos, sprint, gate) MIQCP** with the eTRS-exact node relaxation and the friction-funnel admissible bound driving best-first search (the single-quadratic survey's node oracle, run monolithically instead of split by Benders).

When it would win: on EASY structures where a first incumbent is cheap to find (small n, wide corridors, few edges), the monolith avoids the master/slave round-trip overhead and the IIS bookkeeping, and its single tree shares bound information globally. It is dominated on the hard jumps precisely because cold incumbent finding is the bottleneck there (0 incumbents in 500k nodes), which is what forces the Benders split and the homotopy front end. If a future cold-incumbent primal heuristic made incumbents cheap on hard jumps, the monolith would become competitive again and simpler to maintain.

## Honest caveats

- **Worst-case complexity is exponential in the schedule.** The combinatorial rows (combos, sprint, min-edges) are MILP-hard; H8 legality quotients (sprint latch 2^n -> n+1, edge-cap 9^n -> ~1e8) shrink the reachable space to ~1e8..1e15, and IIS cuts each kill large slabs, but a jump with many near-tied low-edge families can still force many master iterations.
- **eTRS exactness needs the non-intersecting condition or SOC-RLT.** Where two walls cross inside the arc, the raw node bound has a tiny gap; the SOC-RLT rows and arc bisection close it, but this is the one place the "single quadratic => exact" slogan carries an asterisk (single-quadratic survey 2). Verify the node bound is certified on the disk-vs-sphere corpus before trusting the outer loop.
- **Wall homotopy is a path follower with no coverage guarantee.** It can lose the path at a bifurcation (active-set flip) and miss a basin. Mitigate with small rungs near flips and restart on path loss; the Benders certificate still holds for whatever incumbent it produces, but a missed basin can raise the first incumbent's edge count and widen the search.
- **Gate fold can commit prematurely.** A replay that violates a folded gate must reopen it into the 3-way branch, else the affine model is unfaithful and the certificate is void. The premature-commitment guard is mandatory.
- **Global certification is expensive on the hardest ja jumps.** j154 rides four walls at 1e-6 with tightest proven-safe clearance 4e-7; the arc must bisect deep and the SOC-RLT rows must be active for the node bound to certify, so per-node cost and iteration count are both highest exactly where the answer matters most.
- **The certificate is only as exact as `ExactJumpModel`.** gap=0 is against the finite 65536 lattice and the float replica; any divergence of the replica from real MC is a correctness bug that no amount of solver rigor detects. The replay is the truth, and it must stay byte-identical to `SimulatorEntity`.

## Spike validation (2026-08-30): GO-WITH-CAVEATS

A Python spike (SCIP single-quadratic port + the recovered gate-fold + a discrete bucket-walk closer, byte-verified through the Java NoTurnReplay) tested the ruling before the Java build.

RESULT: the hammer is confirmed. j1150 pure was CRACKED COLD, byte-exact: cold disk-relaxed solve finds the diagonal family (theta 20.75), one Java gate-fold refold takes byteViol 2.4e-2 to 1.7e-4, the 65536-grid integer bucket walk plus free-start translation (gate clearance 2e-6) closes to byteViol 0. Java NoTurnReplay certificate X@49 = -2805.299025094122, maxViol 0.0, a PURE no-turn at 3 edges (NONE x3, SA x13, WD x22, W = the human min-edges ideal), sprint latch at 17, with the free start px -2803.100056, pz 4970.339414 JOINTLY optimized and INTERIOR to the box (a box-center/seed start does not satisfy the landing walls, so free-start search was decisive). The monolith stalled cold (0 incumbents in 150 s / 12759 nodes, matching COPT), confirming the Benders split. j154 ja was NOT closed in budget: the gate-fold fixed point oscillated between two patterns and landed the wrong theta basin, confirming the wall-homotopy ladder is required for knife-edge correctness, not just a cheaper first incumbent.

Corrections to the plan the spike forced:
1. The min-edges master must be the DP (min-TV Viterbi) form, and byte-tightening (fold + bucket walk + replay) must live INSIDE the master loop: a plain min-edges MILP returns degenerate 1-edge fat-wall artifacts that never tighten and starves after the first no-good cut. Rank by edges among byte-certified survivors, not by edge count of continuous-feasible points; every master resolve needs a warm start.
2. The discrete 65536-grid bucket walk plus free-start translation is the certificate-producing step, not a small RENS leaf. The continuous node only reaches ~1e-4; the integer walk does the closure and needs its own min-slack solve with a tunable gate clearance (1e-5 left a 1.46e-5 residual across four balanced walls; 2e-6 closed it). Treat the gate epsilon as a first-class knob.
3. Wall homotopy is mandatory for CORRECTNESS on knife-edge ja, not just for a cheaper incumbent. Skipping it put j154 in the wrong theta basin and made the fold fixed point oscillate. Budget the many-small-rung, per-rung-fold ladder as core, and expect j154-class jumps to dominate total cost.

Single most important thing for the Java port: the gate-pattern fold into the linear model (pattern-aware JumpLinearModel + velocityWalls, re-extracted per candidate) paired with the discrete sine-bucket closer, NOT a continuous SLP (which destabilizes at the knife edge because the byte outcome is piecewise-constant per 0.0055 bucket). The single-quadratic geometry is the easy, already-working part; the risk and cost concentrate in the combinatorial master's cold structure discovery and the knife-edge homotopy.

**Stage 2: wall-homotopy continuation front end (`WallHomotopyDriver`), BUILT and PROVEN for the ja close.** The continuation (widen landing walls by a delta ladder, seed low-edge families at fat walls, track incumbents down the ladder, repair infeasible rungs with jump-adjacent single-tick kinks, close at delta=0 via edge-increasing kink completions) closes **j154's knife-edge ja family byte-exact from a coarse windup ancestor**: V6 = `SD x6, S x8, SD, WA, A, WA x11, W` at 6 edges, objective -1599.7000266, violation 0.0, ja engaged on the last setup tick, free start interior. `NoTurnWallHomotopyCrackTest` (VerySlowSolverTests) gates this close. **The remaining gap is cold seed discovery, and it confirms the Benders master is required, not optional.** The fully automated cold path (no coarse seed provided) does NOT reach j154's V6 within a bounded budget, for a precise measured reason: V6's coarse ancestor `SD x6, S x9, WA x13, W` is feasible at the fat wall (delta 0.30), but every cheap oracle ANTI-ranks it. `diskFeasibleTheta` returns NaN for it; the ja homing screen scores it 1.28 (near the worst in its basin) and ranks it 1593 of 4859 SD-first families, because a single-aim screen cannot fly its SD-to-S-to-WA windup. First-key basin coverage (certify a quota from every leading combo) does not help: the needle is not merely SD-first, it is one specific 3-segment shape among ~500, buried below ~1592 dead SD-first lookalikes, and the covered SD-first representatives (`SD x6, W x6, S x6, W x11`) carry the wrong jump-phase geometry and cannot repair into V6. The certifier is the only oracle that ranks the ancestor correctly, and it is too expensive to run on every shape. This is exactly what the ruling predicts: an objective-ranked schedule-level global search (the min-TV Viterbi master with IIS no-good cuts, resolving against the certified slave) is the mechanism that surfaces the right ancestor without enumerating or screening it. The enumeration front end reaches easy structures (j1150); the continuation closes any structure whose coarse ancestor is known (j154 from a windup seed); the Benders master is what discovers the hard structure's ancestor cold.

### Performance: where the time went, measured (2026-09-05)

Everything below was pinned with the harness `NoTurnColdBench` (per-node `[graph]` timings via `-Dpkc.graphTrace=true`, `SolverTrace` via `-Dpkc.solver.trace=<tag>`, JFR via `-PpkcJfr=<file>`), not estimated.

**Baseline, in-game path on j1150 inner (StructurePoolDriver then beam fallback): 80 s.**
- 35 s enumeration: 1.8M `diskFeasibleTheta` calls (the exhaustive 480-point theta grid scan was 28 percent of all JFR samples), 108k byte screens at 72 forwards each (about 10 s).
- 45 s certifies: 6 certifies at about 7.5 s each, 5 of them infeasible and 2 of those sprint-engage twins of the same key schedule.
- A certify ran the whole Optimize(6) graph under a 9 s deadline (GraphRunner keeps 1.5 s for wrapIls, so the cutoff is 7.5 s). Node timings for a FEASIBLE schedule: recedingHorizon 1.3 to 2.0 s (NONE), freeStartImprove x2 0.4 to 2.6 s each (UNCHANGED), then certBnb FOUND in 0.27 s, then ilsPolish 1.0 s and leafSnap 0.2 s on the objective only. For an INFEASIBLE schedule every node ran to the cutoff; nothing proves infeasibility.
- Inside certBnb the feasible line is always produced by the seed phase (ClosedFormSolve, then the GateFoldFinder SLP; about 2.2 s on the knife-edge j154 V6 line), never by the B&B node loop (nodes=0 on every feasible certify observed). On infeasible schedules the SLP runs about 4.2 s without converging and the B&B open set only grows (49 nodes at about 110 ms kernel each, or the 256 node cap).
- The Optimize(6) certify graph was also WEAKER: it reported the byte-exact j154 V6 line infeasible after 7.5 s, while the B&B-only graph certifies it in 2.0 to 2.3 s.
- The certify was single-threaded (99.9 percent of samples on one thread) on 12 cores.
- The j154 fully-cold run (~55 min, 795 certifies at about 4.1 s) was the same certify cost multiplied by the continuation count.

**What changed.**
1. `NoTurnCertifier.searchGraph`: every search certify in the four drivers runs entry -> certBnb -> emit with a 3 s budget (`searchBudgetNanos` on each driver Config; 3 s keeps every feasible line observed, the slowest being V6 at 2.3 s). The graph a caller passes in is now only the polish graph, run once on the accepted schedule (`NoTurnCertifier.polish`, keeps the better feasible objective).
2. `NoTurnParallel`: the pool driver certifies each edge level on a cores-2 thread pool and takes the lowest-index feasible candidate (identical result to the sequential order); the beam ladder, the homotopy seed and repair batches do the same. `FreeStartSolve` static scratch and the `SmoothFaceRecovery` metric cache were made thread-safe for this.
3. `StructurePoolDriver` enumeration: hierarchical 24/6/1 theta grid scan with an early-out when the coarse minimum is far above `diskKeep`, the sprint-engage twin derived as a one-tick delta instead of a full re-sum, byteScreen coarse-to-fine over the same 72 positions with hoisted buffers. Pool contents are the same except rank shuffles inside near-tied clusters (j154 edge level 3, byteViol spread 0.0018).

### In-game wiring status

The **Stratfinder** entry in the View menu opens `StratfinderWindow` (and the Angle Solver window with it, if that was closed); its Search button drives `NoTurnSearchController`, which runs `StructurePoolDriver` as the primary cold solver with `NoTurnFinder` as the beam fallback; both certify through the 3 s search graph in parallel and polish the accepted schedule once through the Optimize(6) graph the controller passes in. Every byte-exact candidate the drivers certify is reported through `Progress.found` while the search runs; the controller keeps them ranked by `NoTurnRanking` in the mode the window selects (same keys are replaced by the better line under that order), the window lists them with a themed progress strip and stage text, clicking a row applies that line to the document immediately, and Cancel stops the drivers but keeps the list. When the search finishes on its own and nothing was clicked, the top-ranked line is applied.

Ranking (2026-09-08). **Easiest** is the default and costs nothing: no jump-angle flick before one, then the fewest input changes (`NoTurnRanking.inputChanges`: key edges including the first press and the air-hold boundary, plus sprint toggles), then the fewest backward ticks, then the smallest turn after the run-up, with the landing offset as the tie-break. **Furthest** ranks by the offset past the goal wall on the objective axis (`NoTurnRanking.goalWall`, the tightest GE wall for MAX or LE wall for MIN on the objective tick; without one the signed objective is used). Because a certified line is feasibility-only, Furthest runs `NoTurnOptimizePass` after the search (or on demand through the Optimize button): every line not yet optimized is polished through `BuiltinGraphs.optimize(budget)` for the per-line budget the window sets (1 to 120 s, default 10 s), up to eight lines in parallel, and the list re-ranks as lines finish; a polish that fails or does not improve keeps the certified line, and `NoTurnCertifier.polish` now rebuilds the spec with the line's air hold so the polished yaws match the keys the controller applies. Lines already optimized are skipped, so switching the budget and pressing Optimize again only touches new lines. `WallHomotopyDriver` and `BendersMaster` are NOT yet wired to the button, so jump-angle structures that need the continuation are solved through the test or harness path. The pool driver's `allowJa` decides whether a jump-angle certify follows a failed pure certify per candidate.

Headless reproduction of the in-game path on any capture: `./gradlew :core:test --tests 'de.legoshi.parkourcalc.anglesolver.NoTurnColdBench' -x tableStyleCheck -Dpkc.bench.capture=hpk/d11/j1150-2x2bm_Nix_Neo -Dpkc.bench.driver=ingame -Dpkc.bench.freeBox=0.8 -Dpkc.bench.allowJa=true -Dpkc.bench.out=<file>` (`driver=human` certifies the recorded keys as a no-turn structure, `driver=keys` a given schedule, `startTick=N` moves the window, `threads=1` forces sequential certifies, `dumpPool=true` writes the ranked pool).

**Human yaws (2026-09-13).** The Stratfinder window has a Human yaws checkbox (on by default, `NoTurnSearchController.playable`, `StructurePoolDriver.Config.playable`, `NoTurnFinder.Config.playable`, bench flag `pkc.bench.playable`). The certify cascade hugs the objective, so every certified line is a vertex of the feasible set: yaws that graze an obstacle by 1e-5 blocks and flick out for one tick and back, which no human can play, while the list ranks by presses and reads the objective only as a late key. With the mode on, `NoTurnCertifier` runs the theta-sweep SLP centered (a seed-taking `SlpSolve.optimizeCentered`, hug fallback when the centered solve cannot reach its clearance), then smooths every certified line with `TrendFilterSmooth` on the pinned scenario, giving back the whole margin above the near landing wall (`landingGiveBack`; a custom-angle objective keeps the default, no landing wall on the objective axis means unbounded), and re-verifies byte-exact; the polish keeps its yaws only when they do not add reversals. `betterResult` ranks fewer turn reversals ahead of the max single-tick turn. `DeWiggle` holds every tick that a facing constraint names, so its repair no longer moves tied ticks into a rejected candidate. Cost is about 0.4 s of smoothing per certified line plus the centered solve; `NoTurnPlayableTest` pins that the playable line stays byte-exact with no more reversals on j1150 and j154.

### The cheap certify hunt (2026-09-05, 50 prototypes on one benchmark)

Question: can a per-candidate feasibility check decide in 0.5 to 3 s what the 5 s search certify decides? Method: a shared benchmark (`scratchpad/fastcheck-cases.jsonl`, 10 known byte-exact feasible lines from the d11 set and j154, 45 pool candidates that neither the 5 s search certify nor the 9 s Optimize certify can solve), a pluggable `FastCheck` interface (`noturn/FastCheck`, `FastCheckVerdict`), and the `NoTurnFastCheckBench` runner that re-verifies every FEASIBLE claim through `ExactJumpModel` and counts hits, misses, false rejects, bogus witnesses and rejects. Ground truth: the search certify hits 10/10 (mean 650 ms, max 2.6 s) and leaves all 45 undecided at 4.3 s mean; the Optimize graph misses V6. Fifty agents each prototyped one approach (48 reported).

What worked (accept side):
- `fastcheck/ThetaSweepAirSlp`: sweep the single setup facing at 1 degree with the run-up rigid and a single-aim air turn, rank thetas by byte-exact violation, run `SlpSolve` on the 20 best with the start pinned to the box centre. 7/10 hits, 47 ms mean, 167 ms max; infeasible candidates give up in 30 ms. Misses the three lines whose start must slide inside the free box.
- `fastcheck/SlpWarmStartDiskTheta`: `GateFoldFinder` SLP (homotopy on) warm-started at the disk theta, 1 s cap (needs the `warmSeed` overload added to `GateFoldFinder.solve`). 6/10 hits at 47 ms median, including the j925 line the B&B seed cannot find; misses the disk-NaN knife-edge lines and stalls at violation 0.0015 on the two optimizer-tight lines.
- The seed phase alone (closed form then SLP) finds 8/10; the B&B node loop never produced a feasible line; `recedingHorizon` is the specialist for start relocation and `freeStartImprove` reaches j925's line in 124 ms.

What did not work: every sound rejection bound rejects 0 of 45 (run-up momentum windows and exact translation rectangles per theta bucket, air reach intervals, per-wall theta intervals, backward reachability, Farkas certificates with a tight bound); the bounds that do reject (disk relaxation Farkas, disk-LP theta scoring, sampled takeoff hulls) false-reject 2 to 10 of the 10 feasible lines because the air-turn slack is a heuristic, not an over-approximation, and the linearized momentum model is off by more than `diskKeep` on knife-edge lines. Per-wall theta intervals measured the gap: the air-reach bound would need to tighten by 0.15 to 0.4 blocks to reject 5 to 16 of the 45 without a false reject. Lattice local search, coordinate descent, Nelder-Mead, differential evolution, random restarts, closed-form-only sweeps, seed caches across candidates, budget escalation, plateau abort, bandit ordering, kernel micro-optimisation, allocation trimming, prefix caching and batched forwards changed no verdict: infeasible candidates are budget-bound, and the certify exposes no graded closest miss (violation is 0 or infinity), so anytime policies have nothing to read. Facing quantisation does not collapse run-up trajectories (one distinct end state per sine bucket, about 182 per degree).

Shipped: `fastcheck/CascadeCheck` (sweep up to 0.4 s, warm SLP up to 0.3 s, then the search graph with the remaining budget) behind `NoTurnCertifier.certifySearch`, which every driver now uses for search certifies. Default search budgets: pool driver, beam finder and Benders master 2 s; the homotopy continuation 3 s. The budget is the verdict: a certify that finds nothing in time counts as infeasible and the search moves on, which can only lose coverage, never accept a wrong line (every witness is byte-exact verified). Measured time-to-hit that fixes those caps (`-Dpkc.graphTrace=true` prints one `[whd]` or `[bm]` line per certify): the 11 feasible rung certifies of the V6 close take 5 to 1401 ms (median 282 ms) and the V6 guard fails at a 2 s rung budget, passes at 3 s in 49 s; the Benders master's feasible fat certify took 473 ms; the benchmark's 10 feasible lines take 21 ms median, 785 ms max at a 2 s budget. The search graph gives certBnb ceil(0.7 x budget) seconds and recedingHorizon ceil(0.4 x budget) under the overall deadline, because integer-second rounding starved the seed at small budgets. An infeasible certify is still a timeout, not a proof.

### Screen-witness warm start (2026-09-07, `fastcheck/ScreenWitnessWarmStart`)

Question: does seeding the certify from the byte screen's own witness (the screen facing on every tied tick, the scanned air facing on the air ticks, both already computed for every pool candidate) compress the feasible tail enough to justify a much shorter certify cap? Measured on the 55-case benchmark above, same machine and 2 s budget, three repeats of the final form:

- 10/10 hits on every repeat, no false rejects, no bogus witnesses; feasible mean 147 ms, median 124 ms, max 330 ms (337 ms across all runs). The shipped cascade on the same machine: 10/10, median 21 ms, max 808 ms, with the two slowest lines (`j1150i-found`, `j1150r-found`) only reached by the search-graph stage at 600 to 800 ms. The screen seed is not faster on the easy lines (the theta sweep hits those in 2 to 30 ms); it removes the tail.
- The seed plus the homotopy is what works. `GateFoldFinder` from the same seed without homotopy misses three j1150 lines (stalls at violations 6.5e-7 to 3.4e-3), and the disk-theta seed of `SlpWarmStartDiskTheta` reached 6/10; the difference is the byte-exact screen facing and the air aim on the air ticks. A byte-exact local snap around the screen witness (theta over plus or minus 64 sine buckets, air facing over plus or minus 2 degrees, the start shift solved exactly as the wall-interval intersection) never closed a line on its own (best gaps 0.02 to 0.67 blocks) and was dropped.
- Infeasible candidates still run to the cap; nothing here rejects. With the 500 ms cap every feasible repeat stayed under 337 ms, so a screen-warm stage capped at 500 ms in place of the 2 s search-graph stage would cut an infeasible pool certify from about 2 s to about 0.55 s (the sweep's give-up plus the cap) with no measured coverage loss on the benchmark. Not wired: the cascade order and the cap are the open decision, and the cap must become a work budget (forward count or SLP calls) before it is trusted under 8-way contention in game.

### Overnight speed campaign (2026-09-08): what shipped and what it measured

Owner's goal: find more byte-exact lines faster; feasibility only. Two waves of targeted agents (six, then seven) each worked one question in its own worktree against a measured gate; the integrator merged the wins by hand and re-gated. Everything below is in the tree; numbers are headless on the 12-core box unless marked.

**Certify path.** `CascadeCheck` is now: joint sound reject (`fastcheck/NoTurnJointFastCheck`, see below) -> theta sweep -> `ScreenWitnessWarmStart` -> search graph if budget remains. `GateFoldFinder.solve` with a warm seed first tries evalAbsolute, translatePolish and a 15 ms enumerateFromSeed on the seed, then a 30 ms probe, then incremental homotopy in batches of five starts with a checkpoint after each (profiling showed 83 percent of warm certify time in `homotopySeeds` while only two of ten lines needed it), and only then the full seed enumeration; the cold path is unchanged (search-graph hits 8 of 10 before and after). Every budget inside `GateFoldFinder`, `ScreenWitnessWarmStart` and the cascade is a `solver/WorkDeadline` that counts thread CPU time instead of wall clock: under 8-way oversubscription the screen-warm check kept 28 to 29 of 30 hits where the wall-clock version kept 12. Screen-warm certify on the 55-case benchmark: 30 of 30, mean 25 to 34 ms, max 100 to 163 ms (start of the campaign: mean 147 ms, max 337 ms).

**A cheap rejection exists now, shipped as a research tool.** `NoTurnJointFastCheck` encloses the run-up facing's (cos, sin) in a rotated arc box inflated by 2e-4 (one sine-table bucket), keeps the start shift inside the free box, relaxes each free air tick's direction to a circumscribed 12-gon, loosens walls by 1e-6, and branch-and-prunes facing intervals down to 0.02 degrees with a small simplex (`LpFeas`); an empty relaxation of the linear model is an infeasibility proof for that model. On the benchmark it rejects 36 of 45 presumed-infeasible candidates with 0 false rejects in about 20 ms; on j1150's real pool it rejected 64 to 97 percent of cold certifies with 0 contradictions over about 1500 audited calls (`fastcheck/JointReject` AUDIT mode certifies anyway and counts rejected-but-feasible candidates, and on a contradiction prints a per-wall diagnosis). It is OFF by default (`-Dpkc.bench.jointMode=audit|enforce` in the harness) for one measured reason: `JumpLinearModel` has no inertia clamp, while `ExactJumpModel` zeroes any per-axis velocity below the threshold (0.005 on 1.8.9) at the top of each tick. On the f2f save, whose setup is idle and airborne with zero start velocity, that discrepancy reached 0.035 blocks at the far wall and produced two false rejects (found with the tolerance-tie slack on; the slack itself was verified exact). The same mechanism can bite any line whose minor-axis velocity component sits under the threshold, for example a flight within about a degree of an axis direction, which the benchmark never exercises, and a globally sound clamp slack (threshold times the coefficient sum per wall) removes every rejection. The open fix is a per-facing-interval clamp slack inside the interval LP; until then enforcing it in game would trade a small first-hit gain (about 1 s on j1150, nothing on marked saves where it abstains) for a silent loss of feasible lines.

### Sound joint rejection (2026-09-08): the inertia clamp as a proof, not a caveat

The review pass left `fastcheck/NoTurnJointFastCheck` bench-only because `JumpLinearModel` has no inertia clamp while `ExactJumpModel` zeroes any per-axis carry velocity below the threshold at the top of a tick (0.005 on 1.8.9, 0.003 per axis on 1.12 and 1.21.3, a combined 9e-6 gate on modern players). This pass makes every rejection of the check a proof for the exact model and measures what that costs. Nothing below changes what the check accepts: a FEASIBLE verdict never comes from it, and UNKNOWN is always allowed.

**The argument.** The exact stepper's clamp at the top of tick s subtracts a velocity c_s from the carry, then adds the tick's input, moves, and multiplies by friction; so c_s enters every later position exactly like a negative input at tick s, and pos_k on that axis equals the clamp-free affine position minus the sum over s < k of c_s times coef(s, k). Two facts bound c_s: it is zero unless the carry is below the threshold (for the combined gate, unless both components are, so the per-axis condition is necessary for it), and when it fires it equals the carry itself, so |c_s| is at most min(threshold, |v_s|). The check therefore needs, per facing interval, a lower and an upper bound on |v_s| for each axis and tick. The clamp-free carry velocity is affine in the same LP variables as the walls (the shared (cos, sin) of the tied run-up, the free directions of the untied ticks), so its range over the interval's inflated arc box, plus a radius for the free directions (their input magnitudes propagated by friction), plus a radius for the model error (below), plus a radius for the clamps that may already have fired (C_t = f_{t-1}(C_{t-1} + rem_{t-1})), gives [lo, hi]; the tick may fire when that interval reaches below the threshold, and its removal bound rem_t is min(threshold, max(|lo|, |hi|)), zero otherwise. An idle start with zero velocity gets rem 0 at every idle tick because the interval is exactly [0, 0]; a start velocity under the threshold gets rem = |v0| at tick 0, which is exactly what the game removes. Each wall row is then loosened by the sum over s < k of |coef(s, k)| times rem_s on its axis; the prefilter uses the same loosened rows, so it never prunes an interval the LP would keep.

That bound alone is too loose where it matters. Once a free-direction tick sits before the wall (the ja jump, or the WA air ticks after takeoff), the free-direction radius makes the carry interval reach zero on both axes for every later tick, and the wall slack grows to 0.005 times the sum of the coefficients: 0.21 blocks at the far wall of a ja candidate, 0.05 to 0.07 blocks for the last five air ticks of a plain no-turn. Measured on the fast-check bench that slack rejects 6 of the 45 presumed-infeasible cases (36 without any clamp slack). The check therefore adds a first-firing case split whenever the full-slack LP is feasible and the zero-slack LP is not: for one axis, every exact trajectory either never fires the clamp on that axis before the last wall (then the axis's wall rows carry no clamp slack at all) or fires it first at some tick t among the ticks the bounds allow (then the clamp-free carry at t satisfies |v_t| < threshold plus the model-error radius, two linear rows in the LP variables, and the axis's wall rows carry only the slack from t onward); the other axis keeps its full slack. If all cases of either axis are empty the interval is empty. The split is run per interval, not only at the leaves, because an infeasible candidate whose full-slack relaxation is feasible over a whole 3 degree arc would otherwise pay for 150 leaves. A nested split over both axes (pairs of first-firing ticks, tried both exhaustively and restricted to the surviving single-axis cases) rejected nothing more on the bench and doubled to tripled the cost, so it is not in the tree. With the single-axis split the bench rejects 20 of 45 (0 false rejects, 0 bogus): all 9 j1149 cases, 3 of the 9 j1150 cases (the unsound version rejected 4), 3 of 6 on j154 and on j716, 2 of 6 on j718, none of the 6 j828 cases (the two-segment jump). Of the 16 rejections the unsound version had and this one has not, 15 are ja candidates (a free jump direction of magnitude 0.33 before ten free air ticks), where the surviving case is a jump that cancels the run-up velocity followed by a slow drift; the relaxation cannot see that such a drift needs the air directions to stay within about 12 degrees of the axis normal every tick, and closing that would need the zeroing-pattern affine model per case (`JumpLinearModel` with a pattern), which is exponential in the air ticks.

**Model-error terms, now computed instead of assumed.** The old flat 1e-6 wall tolerance did not cover two systematic effects. The linear model used the double friction 0.91 while the game multiplies by 0.91F (2.6e-8 relative per tick, compounding to 1e-6 to 3e-6 at a far wall, always in the same direction); the check now builds `JumpLinearModel` with float friction, so the friction chain is identical. The legacy float input chain (about fifteen float roundings between the attribute value and the added velocity) is bounded per tick by 64 float ulps of the tick's input magnitude (`FLOAT_REL`), propagated with the same coefficients as the inputs into both the wall rows and the carry-velocity radius; the sum is 2e-4 to 5e-4 blocks at a far wall. The sine table puts each of cos and sin within one bucket (2 pi / 65536) of the true value, and the cos index is offset before truncation so the two can come from adjacent buckets; the arc box and the chord and tangent rows are inflated by 2.5e-4 (they see up to two buckets). The exact gate evaluates facing constraints on the game facings (`JumpConstraintCompiler.evaluate` wraps `F[t1] - F[t2]`), so an EQ tie with a zero right-hand side forces bit-identical facings and identical table pairs, and the campaign's worry about tied ticks after a gap or a yaw-locked tick is moot: a line whose float facings drifted by an ulp there fails the gate. Two things still separate the pairs of a tied group. The sprint-jump boost uses a different rad cast from moveFlying on 1.8.9, so its pair can sit one bucket from the move pair (0.2 times two buckets times |coef|). And the dF marks of a saved jump do not compile to EQ ties at all: `AngleSolverEngine.addRelative` emits a pair of inequalities with a 1e-4 degree tolerance, which is why the campaign's check abstained on every candidate of the three marked saves ("no tied run-up group") and only ever fired on the unmarked hpk captures. The check now accepts a link as a tie when both a lower and an upper bound on the facing difference exist within 1e-3 degrees (an EQ row, or a GE plus an LE row in either orientation), and charges a band-tied group its summed link bands plus two buckets as a per-tick pair deviation on every tied tick (the facings of two tied ticks differ by at most the band sum, each pair is within one bucket of its own facing); for a group whose links are all exact the deviation stays zero. The check abstains on any surface other than NORMAL before the last wall (ladder, soul sand, water, lava, cobweb are not in the linear model), on a free start velocity and on cross-axis walls; walls with a second tick and facing constraints that are not ties are dropped, which only relaxes. The wall rows are compared against the byte-exact gate `maxViolation <= 0`, so a wall is loosened, never tightened.

### Why no cheap forward oracle is faithful (measured, `ScreenFalseNegativeDiagnosticTest`)

A controlled peel-back on the true-feasible V6 family isolates why every cheap ranker gives false negatives, and rules out the tempting fixes (a finer theta grid, a better free-start model, a takeoff-velocity target). All numbers measured on j154 (n=39, takeoff tick 28, air phase ticks 29-38, four walls at 1e-6).

- **Landing-residual ladder.** V6's screen residual stays large across every cheap-forward rung (shipped byteScreen 3.14, best straight facing over 360 deg 1.14, at the certified theta* 3.42, plus the certified free-start pin 3.42) and collapses to 0.0 only when the full certified post-takeoff air turn and jump-angle are restored (M3). The dominant missing term is the decoupled ~150 degree air turn, NOT the theta window (M0-broad == M1) and NOT the free start (M2 == M1). Worse, the straight-coast screen ANTI-ranks: the infeasible look-alike scores 0.39 while feasible V6 scores 1.14, because V6 needs the biggest air turn, so the screen penalizes exactly the maneuver that makes it feasible.
- **Disk NaN.** `diskFeasibleTheta(V6)` is NaN because the disk's post-setup reach bound `turnSlack` (from air input impulses only) is 1.7x to 9.8x smaller than the actual post-takeoff air-coast reach the certified turn uses (tick30 0.026 vs 0.255, tick34 0.346 vs 1.102, tick39 1.105 vs 1.871). The disk models a pure no-turn coasting straight and omits the ja+turn maneuver; that omission is the whole NaN.
- **Takeoff-velocity oracle is a FILTER, not a ranker.** The feasible takeoff-velocity region R is not razor-thin (velX width 0.036, velZ width 0.108, 22 of 81 cells). V6's takeoff velocity (0.0853, -0.2264) is interior to R, but so is the infeasible look-alike's (0.0451, -0.2414), only 0.043 away and itself air-certify feasible, yet the family is globally infeasible. Velocity-space membership therefore passes a reachable-but-infeasible look-alike and cannot rank the needle above it. The discriminating constraint is not in velocity space: it is the run-up windup coupling, where the single free-start translation must simultaneously satisfy the run-up's own intermediate position boxes (ticks 1, 14, 27) AND the landing corridor. Any velocity abstraction, position-reach cone, or landing screen discards one leg of the run-up-windup / free-start / air-turn / landing-corridor coupling.

Conclusion: separation between feasible V6 and the infeasible look-alikes appears only at the full joint certify, which couples all four legs at once. No faithful cheap forward oracle is possible here; a velocity-space test can pre-filter candidates but cannot replace the objective-ranked Benders master.

### Facing units over every tick (2026-09-13)

The pool driver used to split a jump at the last jump tick (`setupEnd`): keys were enumerated only up to it, the air after it was one held combo with one aimed facing, and an untied tick before it (a jump tick left free for a jump-angle) got a per-wall "free slack" in the screen instead of a facing. The split had no physical reason and every later patch (`flexTick`, `tailDwell`, the two air-hold variants, `airBoundary`, `MultiFacingScreen` for a second tied segment) existed only because of it. It failed for real on `cross4.json` (1.8.9, two jumps at ticks 0 and 13, dF=0 marks on 0 to 12 only): the free slack of the unmarked jump tick, added independently to every wall, made 3873 of 4000 pool candidates screen at violation 0 (byte-exact truth 0.46 to 1.37 blocks), the pool cap trimmed the feasible `Wx8 WDx5 WA` line before certify, and neither the pool nor the beam found anything although the cascade certifies that line in 0.04 s.

Now every tick has a combo and a facing unit (`NoTurnProblem.unit`): ticks tied by the player's marks share one unit, every unmarked tick is its own unit, and `mainUnit` is the unit of the first tied tick. `StructurePoolDriver` enumerates key schedules over all `n` ticks with one alphabet, one edge cap and one dwell rule (a segment may start on any jump tick with any length; a change to a forward combo there costs no edge; the last segment may be shorter than `minDwell`), with three sprint variants per schedule (engage on the first run tick, one and two ticks later). The disk prefilter scans the main unit's facing with the other units folded into a per-wall slack from their movement magnitudes (a relaxation, so it only rejects). `UnitScreen` ranks: it groups ticks into facing groups (each tied unit, each jump tick alone, each run of unmarked ticks between them as one aim), linearizes the byte forward once (the model is affine in every tick's rotated input, so one linearization is exact for any facings up to rounding), sweeps the main group's facing over 36 coarse steps and bisects around the best two, and at every step descends the other groups by coordinate search continued from the neighbouring facing (restarts at a quarter turn apart every sixth step, since coordinate descent on a max of linear walls stalls at kinks); the winner is re-linearized twice. Measured on cross4: the feasible line screens at 0.005 (byte-exact 0.004) and the pool finds `Wx8 WDx5 WAx11` sprint@2 cold at 1.5 s; cw2cw 1.4 s, bfneo 0.5 s, f2f 1.0 s, xxl/60 1.3 s (human family at 19 s), xxl/61 64 s (level 3, 7.8M schedules scored, up from 43 s before the third sprint variant). `screenExact` realizes the witness byte-exactly from the group facings, and `ScreenWitnessWarmStart` seeds the certify from the same facings. Results carry full-length combos, so the Stratfinder writes every row from the line itself and the document's air rows are never consulted. `NoTurnProblem.buildSpec` still accepts a shorter schedule (the research drivers pass run-up-only combos) and fills the rest from the base scenario. Enumeration also honours the driver's total budget, so a level that would not finish in time is cut instead of hanging the search.

### Byte screen, pool ranking and enumeration (2026-09-06)

Pinned with the harness on `cw2cw-noinputssearch.json` (1.8.9 save folder) and the d11 j1150 capture with a 0.8 free-start box:

- `rankPool` ordered the pool by edge count first, so 4000 one- and two-edge candidates that the byte screen already rejected filled the pool cap and the three-edge line the player found never reached certify. The pool now ranks by `certifyTier`: screen-exact candidates (`byteViol <= cfg.byteExact`, 0.01) by edges then violation, then screen-near ones (`<= cfg.byteFeasible`, 0.05) by edges then violation, then the rest by violation alone. The certify loop walks tiers in that order and stops after the first parallel batch that produced a byte-exact result.
- The byte screen simulated the air after the last jump with the run-up facing, so a line that needs the free air turn screened as infeasible (j1150 winner: 0.169 blocks) and the ranking inside the rejected bucket was meaningless. `byteEval` now scans the air facing per theta sample (24 coarse steps, two refine levels) using the `JumpLinearModel` air kernel with per-tick magnitudes from `NoTurnModel.magArg`; that model matches the byte simulation to 1e-5 blocks. The required start shift is `rhs - value(phi)`, so the air delta is subtracted from the shift buffer; adding it made everything screen feasible. `screenExact(combos, sprint, theta)` re-simulates the screen's best point byte-exactly and the harness flag `-Dpkc.bench.screenExact=true` prints screen vs exact violation for a key string.
- `enumerateParallel` splits the enumeration by the first key segment across `NoTurnParallel.resolveThreads(cfg.threads)` worker drivers (each with its own scratch state) and merges the pools; j1150 enumeration went from 33 s to 6.8 s.
- Measured after the changes (12 logical cores, harness, 9 s polish): cw2cw 5.3 s (player line found at 2.1 s), bfneo 7.3 s (found at 0.6 s), j1150 25.8 s (found at 18.9 s). An infeasible candidate still costs the 2 s search budget plus a 2 s jump-angle retry when `allowJa` is on.
- Harness flags added: `pkc.bench.byteStride`, `pkc.bench.screenExact`, `pkc.bench.cascade` (route a keys certify through `CascadeCheck` exactly as the pool does).
- Free-direction setup ticks (a jump tick left unmarked so the player may turn on it, or a second dF segment) used to take their prefilter and byte-screen slack from the base scenario's magnitude at that tick. With the inputs wiped the base magnitude is zero, so the takeoff tick's entire movement vanished from the model and every schedule failed the 0.25-block disk cutoff (`f2f-noinputssearch.json`: 29922 scored, 0 byte-screened). The slack is now computed per candidate from the candidate's own combo and sprint state (`computeFreeSlack`, `Group.coefFree`, `Group.freeSlack`); the same wiped save now solves cold in 11 s with a one-edge line. Harness flag `-Dpkc.bench.dumpBase=true` prints the per-tick base scenario to catch this class of base-dependence.
- Minimum dwell versus the takeoff: `enumSeg` required every key segment to last `minDwell` (3) ticks, including the final one. When the marks tie the jump tick and the first air tick (setupEnd one past the jump), a key change on the jump tick becomes a two-tick segment and is unenumerable; the wiped f2f variant with that structure had no feasible schedule in the pool at all (420 certifies and the beam fallback all failed). The driver now records `flexTick`, the last jump tick strictly inside the enumerated range, and lets a segment start there with any length while the segment before it may end short of the dwell. Enumeration grows by about one percent on j1150; that variant now solves cold in 11.4 s with the player's own two-edge line.
- Minimum dwell versus the landing (2026-09-10): xxl/61-no-find (two jumps, marks through the air, landing on tick 22) had no pool line at all. Its feasible lines all end with a 2-tick WA strafe before landing (`S WAx7 Wx12 WAx2`, 3 edges), and the dwell rule rejected any final segment shorter than `minDwell`. `Config.tailDwell` (default 1) now lets only the last segment of the enumerated range be short; every other segment keeps `minDwell`. Measured on that save: level 3 enumeration 5.9M to 9.2M scored, first line at 46 s (was nothing in 67 s); j1150 pool gate unchanged in result, 21.6 s wall. Dwell 1 everywhere would cost 24M scored at level 3 (99 s) for the same line. The human line there also has a 1-tick idle after the backward hop, which stays unenumerable and is not needed.
- Simplicity ranking (2026-09-06): the pool ranked by transitions between ticks, so a schedule that starts by holding S costs nothing while `-x4 WDx9 W` counts two changes; with the takeoff-dwell relaxation the one-tick `S WDx13` came first on the f2f structure with marks through the jump tick. `NoTurnKeys.countPresses` counts the first press from idle as a change and `countBackward` counts ticks with a backward key. The screen tiers are keyed by presses; inside a tier the order is bucketed violation (0.005 blocks), then backward ticks, then exact violation, so the backward preference only breaks near-ties. `betterResult` (batch winner and the Stratfinder list) prefers fewer presses, then fewer backward ticks, then pure no-turn, then objective. The Stratfinder table shows presses. j1150's line with 16 SA ticks now ranks behind three-press lines with fewer backward ticks, which costs time there; it is a deliberate trade for simpler lines first.
- Parallel certify contention (2026-09-06): the certify budget is wall-clock, and ten parallel certifies plus the game's render thread can starve a line that certifies in 0.1 s alone. On the f2f save with marks through the jump tick, the two leading `-x4 WDx9 W` variants timed out inside the first batch in one of three headless runs, and the batch then fell through to `SA WDx12 W`. `run` now re-certifies, alone and with double budget, up to five higher-ranked candidates that returned nothing when a lower-ranked one in the same batch passed, on screen-exact levels only (near-tier timeouts are usually real misses and the retry there cost 8 s on f2f2). Certify threads are capped at eight; enumeration keeps the full worker count.
- Presses over the whole line (2026-09-06): the player's `WDx10 WA` (WA held into the air) and the search's `-x4 WDx9 W` (D released on the jump tick, A pressed one tick later for the default WA air) tied at two run-up presses, but the second is three key events once the air boundary counts. `fullPresses` adds the transition from the last setup combo to the base air combo; `Candidate.presses` and `NoTurnResult.pressCount` carry it and the Stratfinder column shows it. Tiers are now keyed by presses first and screen-exact/near second (`2 * presses + near`), so a simpler near-miss line is certified before a more complex screen-exact one. After the first hit the loop keeps certifying candidates with the same press count for up to `extraCertify` (30) certifies or `extraCertifyNanos` (8 s) so alternatives reach the list. With `allowJa` the pool is walked twice, pure first and jump-angle second, instead of retrying jump-angle per candidate; the second pass gets its own certify cap.
- Exploring after the first hit (2026-09-06): the player wants variations, not only the first feasible line. After the first byte-exact hit the certify loop now keeps walking the ranking across all tiers, streaming every certified line through `Progress.found`, until `cfg.extraCertify` candidates or `cfg.extraCertifyNanos` have been spent or the search is cancelled; the harness default stays small (30 / 8 s) so the gate tests stay fast, and `NoTurnSearchController` raises it to 200 candidates / 45 s in game. The auto-applied line is still the simplest by `betterResult`. Harness flags `pkc.bench.extraCertify` and `pkc.bench.extraSec`; the harness now logs each streamed result as `[pool] result:`.
- Air keys after the last jump (2026-09-06): they used to come from the document's rows, so leftover W rows forced W in the air and the solver compensated with a 103 degree flick on the first air tick (`bfneo-test.json`); only fully empty air rows got the WA default. Each schedule is now screened in two air variants: hold the takeoff combo through the air (`Candidate.airHold`, `NoTurnProblem.airCombo`, five-argument `buildSpec`) or keep the document's air keys. The pool ranks both in the same press tier with the document-air variant behind (`boundary` tie-break after backward keys), `betterResult` prefers fewer run-up presses, fewer backward ticks, no air boundary, then the smaller maximum single-tick turn (`maxTurnDeg`), then pure no-turn, then objective. `NoTurnResult.airCombo` carries the held combo and `NoTurnSearchController.apply` writes it into the air rows; the Stratfinder shows presses including the boundary. Measured cold with the in-game exploration: f2f `-x3 WDx10 WA` first (25 variants in 62 s), bfneo `- WDx12 WA` first (42 variants), j1150 72 s with its W-takeoff WA-air line.
- Key changes on the space press are free (2026-09-09, owner ruling): "WAD" (strafe held through the run-up, W alone on the jump tick, the opposite strafe held in the air) is the most common human rhythm because pressing space is the cue to release the strafe. The pool driver charged it as two edges plus the first press, so `xxl/60-no-find.json` (1.12.2, dF=0 marks on every tick, human line `-x4 WDx9 W WAx6 Wx5`) needed 4 edges against a cap of 3 and the in-game chain found nothing cold; every 3-or-fewer-edge simplification is infeasible by 0.009 to 0.15 blocks on Z@13, and raising the cap to 4 found it at 11.5 s but scored 21.9M schedules instead of 1.8M. Now `enumSeg` charges no edge for a combo change that lands on a jump tick (`problem.jump[t]`, the takeoff included) and lets a segment start on any jump tick with any length (the old single `flexTick`); the level filter, `Candidate.edges`/`presses`, `fullPresses`, `PoolCertifier.bind` and the beam's edge count use `NoTurnKeys.countEdges(combos, jump)` / `countPresses(combos, jump)`, `betterResult` compares `NoTurnRanking.keyChanges` (the bound press count), and the Stratfinder tooltips state the rule. A change on any other tick is charged as before; the global cap stays at 3.
