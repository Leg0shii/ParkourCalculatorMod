# Seam smoothness: why the first ticks of a solve were choppy, and what changed

Investigation (2026-08-11) into gh-296: TASes come out un-smooth, and the first couple ticks of a fresh solve, or the ticks right after a segment boundary, are choppier than the rest. This note records what the code showed, the two mechanical fixes that shipped with it, and the levers that were deliberately left alone.

## What the code showed

**The shipped jerk metric never saw the seam.** `Angles.wiggleDeg` (the L1 second difference the `Smooth (TAS)` toggle scores through `Objective.smoothPenalty`) started its loop at the second in-window delta and never received the pre-solve facing. With n solved ticks, the turn out of the previous tick's facing and the turn into tick 1 carried exactly zero smoothness cost, at any lambda. That is the literal mechanism behind "it doesn't care for the first couple ticks". `SmoothingPolish.roughness` was the only component that anchored at `startYaw`; the search metric that picks the basin did not.

**No continuity constraint could even be authored at the seam.** A dF constraint on the solve's first tick was silently dropped in `addMapped` (`segTick < 1` bailed out), because `JumpConstraint` Mode F only indexes facings inside the window; there was no symbol for the pre-window facing. The one workaround was hand-typing an absolute F band at tick 0.

**Between segments the record already names the culprit.** `LongRunSolver` chains the last committed game facing into the next window's `startYaw`, so continuity is represented, but every window objective is constructed lambda-free on purpose (the feasibility firewall). `docs/research/data/matrix-step6-finale/analysis.md` measured this: the residual jag on long runs is generated by the receding-horizon windows themselves, and making them lambda-aware collides with the firewall and needs a design session, not a patch.

**The issue's two observations reproduce from the record.** Tighter constraints look smoother because closed-form-territory problems come out with zero direction reversals; the wiggle lives in the slack ticks that CMA-ES and the recovery chains exploit (`matrix-a8base1/analysis.md`: "wiggle is structural", "more optimization makes it worse"). Short solves look smoother because they stay in closed-form or single-window territory and, historically, because the improve chain was tick-capped at 64 (now 256, `BuiltinGraphs.IMPROVE_TICK_CAP`).

## What changed with gh-296

1. **Anchored jerk.** `Angles.wiggleDeg(anchorYaw, f)` treats the pre-solve facing as the delta base, so the first two in-window deltas now carry jerk cost. `Objective.smoothPenalty` and `Objective.scored` require the anchor, and every scoring site passes its scenario's `startYaw`: the CMA-ES fitness, bucket ascent, ILS, ALM/snap grading, the smoothing polish ratchet, `SolveCore`'s candidate ranking, the graph scoring paths, and the `SolveProgress` incumbent gate. (The CMA-ES fitness, ALM/snap grading, and `SolveCore` sites were removed shortly after, PRs 373/375/382; the anchor convention survives at every remaining site: bucket ascent, ILS, the smoothing polish, graph scoring, and `SolveProgress`.) With `Smooth (TAS)` off (lambda 0) nothing changes.

2. **dF at the solve's first tick.** `addMapped` now compiles a dF constraint on the first tick into an absolute F band at tick 0 shifted by the seed facing (`addSeamDeltaFacing`), instead of dropping it. The result panel reports it like any other dF row (`findValue` measures `wrap(F[0] - seedYaw)`). So "turn at most X degrees out of the approach" is now something the user can constrain directly, which is the requested "check the ticks prior and use them" in constraint form.

## What was left alone, and why

- **Receding-horizon windows stay lambda-free.** The firewall exists so lead-in windows never trade feasibility for anything. The matrix record explicitly parks seam-aware window objectives as a design session; this note does not reopen it.
- **The recorded jerk stat stays unanchored.** `SolveRunRecord.smoothnessOf` and the result panel's "Yaw jerk" keep the old definition so the numbers stay comparable with the `docs/research/data/matrix-*` corpus. The gate and the stat therefore measure slightly different things at the seam; that widens the already-open "mixed yaw arrays" item below, knowingly.
- **The mixed yaw-array unification stays open.** Some `smoothPenalty` sites feed base solver yaws, others realized game facings (`matrix-step6-finale/analysis.md`, OPEN AND BLOCKING). The anchor works with both, but smooth mode still slightly taxes realized 45-flicks at the sites that pass game facings. Unifying on base yaws remains the recorded next step.
- **Velocity is still never pinned at seams.** Negative result 1 in `angle-solver.md` §4 stands: seam smoothing must chain exact state, not constrain it. The new seam dF band constrains the facing only.
